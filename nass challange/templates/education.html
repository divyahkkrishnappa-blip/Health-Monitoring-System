Five hazards of spaceflight.

Space exploration introduces unique and interacting health challenges.

SPACE RADIATION

Beyond Earth's protective bubble

Space radiation includes energetic particles from the Sun and galactic sources. Exposure beyond low Earth orbit is an important human research concern; mission shielding and operational planning help manage risk.

Why it matters

NASA studies potential effects on tissues and organs and develops strategies to characterize and reduce exposure. This prototype does not measure radiation.

NASA: Human spaceflight hazards
02
ISOLATION & CONFINEMENT

Small crews, close quarters

Crews live and work in confined environments, often with limited privacy and distance from family. These conditions can affect sleep, mood, teamwork, and performance.

Why it matters

Regular self-reflection, supportive team routines, and mission-designed behavioral health resources are important considerations. Individual experiences differ.

NASA HRP: Hazards of human spaceflight
03
DISTANCE FROM EARTH

Care with a communication delay

As missions travel farther from Earth, communication delays increase and real-time support becomes more difficult. Crews may need greater autonomy and onboard capabilities.

Why it matters

Communication and medical planning are mission-level systems. The delay displayed in AstroHealth is simulated and does not connect to NASA or a spacecraft.

NASA: Human research program
04
GRAVITY FIELDS

Living outside Earth's gravity

Microgravity changes how the body adapts, including systems involved in balance, bones, and muscles. Transitions between gravity environments can also challenge the body.

Why it matters

NASA research examines these changes and countermeasures such as mission-planned exercise. The body explorer offers general information, not personal recommendations.

NASA: Risks of spaceflight
05
CLOSED ENVIRONMENTS

A habitat that supports life

Spacecraft are isolated, engineered environments. Air quality, microbes, noise, lighting, and other environmental factors require careful monitoring and system design.

Why it matters

Environmental monitoring is distinct from the health measurements shown by this prototype. AstroHealth has no sensor access to habitat systems.
